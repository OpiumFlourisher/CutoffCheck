CutoffCheck v1.2

I kept downloading "lossless" albums that turned out to be MP3s with a .flac name, so I made this. It checks if a FLAC is really lossless, fake hi-res or a converted MP3/AAC. Everything stays on your phone, nothing gets uploaded.

tip: tap anywhere on a spectrogram to jump to that part of the song :)

You can pick lots of songs at once. Each one takes about 5–15 seconds on a phone. Tap ▶ Play to listen, and tap the spectrogram to jump to that spot.

FAQ

How can it tell a 320 kbps MP3 from real lossless?

MP3, AAC and Vorbis encoders save the sound in fixed-size blocks, and lots of values inside those blocks end up exactly zero. Those zeros are still there after converting to FLAC. The app redoes each encoder's maths at every possible block position, and if one position shows a big pile of zeros all through the song, it went through that encoder. Real lossless audio never has a position like that.

Is "fake hi-res" bad?

Not for listening. It's real lossless audio at CD quality that someone upsampled to 96 kHz, so it sounds the same and just takes twice the space.

Why is it slow?

It decodes the whole song and runs a few thousand transforms on it, all on your phone. That's the price of not uploading anything.

Does it work offline?

Yes, completely. The app doesn't even have internet permission, so your songs can't leave the phone.

Known issues

  • If a lossy file was resampled after converting (like an MP3 made into fake 96 kHz), only the cutoff check can catch it.
  • Very quiet or very sparse recordings, and songs that clip constantly, can hide the encoder pattern.
  • WMA, AC-3 and other rare codecs are only caught if they leave a cutoff.
  • Big 96 kHz files use a lot of memory on older phones. Check them a few at a time.
